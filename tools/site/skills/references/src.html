Reference words

A pronoun points back to something. Follow the arrow, write what is at the end of it, and the quarter marks add up.

On the paperBold words in the text

F. What does each of the pronouns, bold-typed in the selection above, refer to? 1. where (Paragraph 6) 2. It (Paragraph 9)LS, June 2019, (0.5). Some years ask for four words, (01).

How it’s markedA quarter each

Each reference word is worth 0.25. The official key for June 2019 shows what is accepted: “where” refers to simple childhood (or childhood), and “It” refers to the idea of committing more funding for research. So a shorter form of the right noun is accepted, and it can stand for a whole idea. The wrong noun never earns the mark.

The methodLook back, test, write

KindWordsWatch out
Personalhe, she, it, they, him, them, his, its, their…it can stand for a whole idea, not only a noun.
Relativewho, whom, whose, which, that, where, whenThe antecedent is right before it: the room where…
Demonstrativethis, that, these, thosethis often sums up the previous sentence.
Indefiniteone, each, some, both, many, several, none…Check singular or plural.
  1. Look back: the antecedent is usually in the same or the previous sentence.
  2. Check agreement: singular or plural, person or thing, place or time.
  3. Test it: put your answer in place of the pronoun. If the sentence still makes sense, you are right.
