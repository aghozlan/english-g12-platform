Correcting false statements

Each false statement is a true idea from the text with one thing changed. Find the change, put it right, and the half mark is yours.

On the paperSame task, different letters

B. The following statements are false because they misinterpret the meaning stated or conveyed in the selection above. Rewrite them correctly.LS, June 2019. Earlier papers put it in Section D or E: “…because they misinterpret the writer’s ideas. Rewrite them correctly.”

How it’s markedFix only what is wrong

Statement: Diabetes 1 can be cured by regular doses of insulin.

Official correction (LS 2019): Diabetes 1 can be treated by regular doses of insulin. 0.5

One word was wrong, so one word changed. When a statement can be fixed in two ways, the scheme accepts both, for example “during the day and night” or “more concerned … during the night”.

The methodThe six traps

TrapWhat changed
AntonymA word replaced by its opposite: later → earlier, harder → easier.
TenseThe time moved: is falling asleep → used to fall asleep.
General ↔ specific“some” becomes “all”, “often” becomes “always”, “only” is added.
Cause ↔ effectThe result is presented as the cause.
Wrong personAn act or an opinion is given to someone else.
Wrong orderThe sequence of events is changed: before ↔ after.
  1. Find the sentence in the text that the statement is built on.
  2. Put them side by side and spot the one difference.
  3. Rewrite the whole statement with that part corrected. No “This is false because…”.

The six traps

Antonym, tense, general/specific, cause/effect, wrong person, wrong order.

Absolute words

all, always, every, only, never replacing some, often, a few: the general/specific trap.

The correction

The whole statement, rewritten, with only the wrong part fixed.
