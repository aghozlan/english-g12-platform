Character sketch

A character is revealed by what they do, what they say, and what others say about them. Build your sketch from evidence, not impressions.

On the paperReal questions

Paragraph 7 sheds light mostly on James Cooney’s character. How does the writer describe him throughout the text? (Score: 1.5)LS paper
Draw a character sketch of … as portrayed in the above text.Typical form, from the course notes

How it’s markedThe official split

Marks go to each trait with its evidence. One adjective without proof earns little; two or three traits, each tied to an act or a saying, earn the full mark.

The methodHow to answer

  1. Background: name, age, position or job.
  2. Acts: what does the character do?
  3. Sayings: what do they say, and how?
  4. Others: what do other people say about them?
  5. Result: what happens because of their acts? Then name traits in pairs of opposites: knowledgeable or ignorant, daring or cowardly, tolerant or dogmatic, trustworthy or hypocritical.

See it doneWorked answers

Question. Draw a character sketch of Karim as portrayed in Wired Awake.
Karim is a seventeen-year-old student from Saida. At first he appears weak-willed, since he keeps promising himself “just one more video” at 1:40 a.m. However, he is honest about his struggle, admitting that the first nights without his phone “were terrible”. Above all, he is determined: he keeps the phone basket for a month and is rewarded with better sleep and rising grades.

Background + traits, each tied to an act or saying.

Question. Draw a character sketch of Rana as portrayed in The Price of a Click.
Rana is a nineteen-year-old business student in Tripoli. At first she is impulsive, sharing a shocking photo “at once” without checking it. Yet she is responsible enough to learn from her mistake, and she shows initiative: she now runs a fact-checking page with three classmates, helping other students avoid the same error.

Background + traits with evidence.

Texts: Wired Awake (LS) and The Price of a Click (SE), in the reading room.
