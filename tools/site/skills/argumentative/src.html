Argumentative essay

The most frequent prompt on past papers. You take a side, prove it, and then defeat the other side. The refutation is not optional: the prompt asks for it.

On the paperReal prompts

Some people believe that technology stands behind many dangerous diseases; however, others believe that technology has helped doctors diagnose and treat many diseases. Where do you stand? Write a well-organized argumentative essay of 250-300 words in which you show your point of view. Make sure that your essay supports your position and refutes the opposite point of view.LS, June 2019
Some people believe that the rich should pay more taxes … Others believe that rich people should not be taxed more because they have worked hard for their money … In a well-organized argumentative essay of 250-300 words, show with which point of view you stand.SE, June 2019

How it’s markedContent 3.5, language 3.5, tidiness 1

The last sentence of every argumentative prompt sets the content mark: supports your position and refutes the opposite point of view. An essay that only argues one side is answering half the prompt, however well it is written.

The outlineFour or five paragraphs, each with one job

  1. I. Introduction
    • 1. General statement (hook)
    • 2. Narrower or background statement
    • 3. Thesis statement: your claim and the counter claim
  2. II. Your claim
    • Topic sentence: the point of view you support
    • a. b. c. Three supporting details
    • Concluding sentence
  3. III. The counter claim
    • Topic sentence: the opposite point of view
    • a. b. c. Each of their points, followed by your refutation
    • Concluding sentence
  4. IV. Conclusion
    • Restatement of the thesis and final thoughts

The outline printed with Prompt A of the LS paper, June 2019 (special-needs version).

Many teachers prefer a five-paragraph form: three arguments in three paragraphs, then one paragraph for the counter-argument and its refutation. Both shapes answer the prompt. With 250–300 words, the four-paragraph outline above leaves more room for each point.

TechniqueThe refutation in three moves

  1. Concede: state their view fairly. Admittedly, some argue that…
  2. Counter: show why it is weak, incomplete or wrong. However, these harms come from the misuse of technology, not from technology itself.
  3. Conclude: bring it back to your claim. Therefore, blaming technology for disease is like blaming a knife instead of the hand that holds it.
