Logical fallacies

Some arguments sound convincing but are built on faulty reasoning. Spot the flaw, name it, and say what is wrong.

On the paperReal questions

Identify the logical fallacy in the following statement, and then explain why the reasoning is faulty.Typical form, from the course notes. Not found in the 2004–2019 LS and SE papers on CRDP, but part of the curriculum.

How it’s markedThe official split

Expect the usual split: name the fallacy, then explain the flaw. Reasonable thinking needs reasonable language: beware of always, never, all, none, undoubtedly, obviously.

The methodHow to answer

FallacyExample from the course notes
Faulty causeHe was sure he would do well because everybody wished him good luck.
Hasty generalisationAll smokers die of lung cancer.
False cause from sequence (it happened first)He gets full marks because he smokes a cigarette before every test.
Oversimplification (one cause for a complex problem)The operation succeeded because they used sterilised tools.
Half-truthThe new recycling law is bad because it will cost more money than it saves.
Appeal to ignoranceShow me one study that proves seat belts save lives.
Bandwagon (everybody does it)I choose to accept his proposal because everybody does.
False authorityA gifted accountant, he will obviously be an active member of parliament.

See it doneWorked answers

Question. Identify the fallacy: “My cousin failed the official English exam, so the exam is impossible to pass.”
This is a hasty generalisation. The speaker draws a conclusion about all students from one case. Thousands of students pass every year, so one failure proves nothing about the exam.

Name + why the reasoning fails.
