Figures of speech

The 2026–2027 list requires four: simile, metaphor, personification and irony. Identify the figure, explain what it means, and say why the writer used it.

On the paperReal questions

Explain the metaphor used in Paragraph 9, and show the purpose it serves. (01)LS paper
Identify the ironical situation in Paragraphs 1 and 2. Justify your answer. (Score: 01pt)LS paper
Identify the paradox in Paragraph 1 and explain it. (01)LS, June 2018

How it’s markedThe official split

0.5 for identification and 0.5 for explanation (June 2018, paradox): Biological intelligence is considered a “new” concept although it is very old; logically, something which is four billion years old should be old, not new.

The methodHow to answer

FigureDefinitionExample
SimileA comparison using like or as“It works like a slot machine.”
MetaphorSays one thing is another“The media can be a window on the world or a mirror.”
PersonificationHuman qualities given to something not human“…where it cannot call for attention” (a phone calling)
IronyThe opposite of what is said or expected: verbal, dramatic, or of situationStudents stay up to study, “only to forget” what they reviewed.

Recognise these too, as past papers have used them: paradox (seems contradictory but makes sense), hyperbole (exaggeration), rhetorical question (no answer expected), oxymoron (“hot ice”), alliteration, antithesis.

Purposes: make an idea vivid or concrete, emphasise it, attract attention, express the writer’s attitude.

See it doneWorked answers

Question. Identify the figure of speech in “Yet a phone should be a tool that we use, not a master that decides when we sleep” (Wired Awake, paragraph 9) and explain its purpose.
It is a metaphor: the phone is compared to a master who controls the teenager, a servant. It emphasises how completely phones now rule teenagers’ nights, and warns readers to take control back.

0.5 identification, 0.5 meaning and purpose.

Question. Identify the figure of speech in paragraph 6 of The Price of a Click and explain it.
It is irony of situation: this generation has more information than any before it, yet it may be less informed. The contrast emphasises that quantity of news is not the same as understanding.

0.5 + 0.5.

Texts: Wired Awake (LS) and The Price of a Click (SE), in the reading room.
