Inferring

An inference is not a guess. It is a clue in the text plus something you know, said out loud.

On the paperInfer, suggest, imply

Based on Paragraphs 8 and 9, infer one medical and one governmental measure that could reduce the suffering of children with Diabetes 1.LS, June 2019, Section A, (01)

Other forms: What does … suggest about …? What can you infer about the writer’s attitude? What is implied by …?

How it’s markedThe idea, not the wording

For the 2019 question, the official answer described devices that free children from testing and injecting themselves, and more government funding for research: 0.5 for each. Neither idea is written in those words in the text. That is what makes it an inference.

The methodClue + knowledge = inference

  1. Find the clue: a detail, an action, a word choice in the named paragraph.
  2. Add what you know about people and the world.
  3. State the meaning clearly, and show the link: “Since …, we can infer that …”.
Test: can you point to the clue? If not, it is a guess, and a guess earns nothing.

Check yourself

Clue (Wired Awake, P1): “Just one more video,” he tells himself, as he did an hour ago. What can you infer about Karim?

Clue (P8): the first nights were “terrible”, but by the second week his grades had begun to rise. What does the writer want us to infer?

Clue (The Price of a Click, P1): “Shocked, she shared it at once with a warning to her friends.” What can you infer about Rana?

Clue (P8): “Today she runs a small fact-checking page.” What does this suggest?
