When half a country is held back

Model essay written for this platform, 265 words.

In many countries, women make up half of the population but only a small share of decision-makers. This gap is not only unfair; it is costly. Gender inequality creates serious economic and social problems, but practical steps taken by both citizens and governments can solve them.

The first problem is economic. When women are kept out of good jobs or paid less for the same work, a country loses the talent of half its workforce, and its growth slows down. The second problem is social. When few women hold leadership positions, issues such as health, education and child support receive less attention. As a result, families suffer, and children grow up with fewer opportunities.

Citizens can begin to change this situation at home and at work. Parents can give their daughters the same education and encouragement they give their sons. Employers can promote people according to their skills, not their gender, and offer flexible hours to working mothers. Moreover, men and women alike can vote for qualified female candidates and support them once they are elected.

The government, for its part, must protect equality through law. It can enforce equal pay for equal work and punish discrimination. It can also provide affordable childcare, so that mothers do not have to choose between a career and their children. Finally, scholarships can prepare more women for leadership.

In conclusion, gender inequality weakens both the economy and society, but it is not a fate we must accept. If families, employers and the state work together, women will contribute fully, and the whole country will share in their success.
