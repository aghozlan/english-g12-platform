Technology and disease

Model essay written for this platform, 294 words.

Few inventions have changed human life as deeply as technology. From the phone in our pocket to the scanner in a hospital, machines now shape how we live, work and even fall ill. Although some people blame technology for many dangerous diseases, I firmly believe that it has done far more to help doctors diagnose and treat illness than to cause it.

To begin with, technology allows doctors to discover diseases early, when they are easiest to cure. Scanners reveal a tumour long before the patient feels any pain. In addition, technology makes treatment safer and more precise. Surgeons now use tiny cameras and robotic arms, so operations that once needed weeks in hospital are done in a day. Finally, technology carries medicine to people who could not reach it before. A villager in Akkar can consult a specialist in Beirut through a video call. Clearly, technology has become one of medicine’s strongest allies.

Admittedly, some argue that technology causes serious diseases. They point to radiation, to factory pollution and to the long hours young people spend in front of screens. However, these harms come from the misuse of technology, not from technology itself. Radiation in hospitals is carefully measured and controlled, and the same science that pollutes also gives us filters and clean energy. As for screen addiction, it is a habit that education and self-discipline can correct. Therefore, blaming technology for disease is like blaming a knife instead of the hand that holds it.

In conclusion, technology has transformed the way doctors detect and treat disease. Its risks are real, but they can be controlled when it is used wisely. If we invest in medical technology and teach people to use it responsibly, the future of health will be brighter than ever.
