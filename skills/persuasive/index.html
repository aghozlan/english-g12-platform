Persuasive essay

You already hold the view the prompt gives you. Your job is to make the reader feel it is true, with strong reasons, real examples, and a closing call to act.

On the paperReal prompts

“Overreliance on online resources to learn results in a world of depersonalized communication.” Write a persuasive essay of 250-300 words in which you show how important it is to have face-to-face communication in order to build social skills and family relationships (team work, communication, values and manners, social responsibilities, tolerance, etc.)SE, August 2019
To sustain the beauty and value of nature, all living things should be well-preserved. Write a persuasive essay of 250-300 words in which you show how vital it is to preserve nature and protect its living things.LS, July 2019, second session

How it’s markedContent 3.5, language 3.5, tidiness 1

Persuasive prompts usually give you the two controlling ideas: social skills and family relationships above. Those become your two body paragraphs. The ideas in brackets are a gift: use them as evidence. No counter-argument is needed.

The outlineFour or five paragraphs, each with one job

  1. I. Introduction
    • A. Hook
    • B. A narrower statement linking the hook to the thesis
    • C. Thesis statement with your two controlling ideas
  2. II. Topic sentence 1: the first controlling idea
    • A. B. C. Three pieces of supporting evidence (details and examples)
    • D. Concluding sentence
  3. III. Topic sentence 2: the second controlling idea
    • A. B. C. Three pieces of supporting evidence
    • D. Concluding sentence
  4. IV. Conclusion
    • A. Restatement of the thesis
    • B. Final thought

Face to face

Model essay written for this platform, 264 words.

How many times have you sat at a family dinner where everyone was looking at a screen? Online resources have made learning faster and communication easier than ever. Yet a world that talks only through screens risks forgetting how to talk at all. That is why face-to-face communication remains essential, both for building social skills and for keeping families close.

First of all, real conversations teach social skills that no screen can teach. When students work together in the same room, they learn to listen, to take turns and to settle disagreements politely. Moreover, they learn to read faces, tones and gestures, which carry much of what people mean. A shy student who presents in front of classmates gains a confidence that a typed message will never give him. In short, face-to-face contact turns knowledge into teamwork.

Equally important, face-to-face communication is the heart of family life. Values such as respect, patience and generosity are passed on at the dinner table, not through a group chat. When parents and children talk directly, misunderstandings are cleared quickly, and young people feel heard. Grandparents, too, can share stories and traditions that would otherwise be lost. Families that make time to sit together are, quite simply, families that stay together.

To sum up, online resources are useful, but they must never replace the human voice and the human face. Personal communication builds the skills we need at school and the bonds we need at home. So put the phone down tonight and start a real conversation: it may be the most important lesson you learn this year.

TechniqueTools of persuasion

  1. A question that involves the reader: How many times have you sat at a family dinner where everyone was looking at a screen?
  2. Concrete examples the reader recognises: the shy student presenting to the class, grandparents telling stories.
  3. Facts and figures when you have them, and confident, positive wording: essential, the heart of, never.
  4. A call to act in the final thought: So put the phone down tonight…
