Drawing a conclusion from a table or graph

Section D gives you a table or graph linked to the text. The answer always has three parts: what the table shows, the evidence, and the conclusion.

On the paperReal questions

The table below shows the percentages of prevalence of Diabetes 1 by age. Read it carefully, and then answer the question that follows. What conclusion can be drawn from the table? Explain your answer in 4 to 5 sentences, using evidence. (1.5)LS, June 2019

How it’s markedThe official split

Official split (June 2019): 0.5 for the introductory sentence, 0.5 for the explanation with evidence, and 0.5 for the concluding sentence. The official answer began: The table provides statistics about the relation between age and the percentages of Diabetes 1… and ended: Thus, the older the person gets, the higher the risk of Diabetes 1 will be.

The methodHow to answer

  1. Read the title, the labels and the scale. What is measured, in what unit?
  2. Introductory sentence: “The table entitled … shows …”.
  3. Evidence: the lowest and highest values, and the trend between them, with the numbers.
  4. Conclusion: one sentence that states the relation: “Thus, the more …, the …”. Link it to the text if you can.

For a picture or illustration: describe it, relate it to the text, then draw a conclusion.

Question. What conclusion can be drawn from the table? Explain in 4–5 sentences, using evidence.
The table shows how much teenagers sleep on school nights according to the time they spend on screens after 8 p.m. Those who use screens for less than an hour sleep 8.6 hours, which is within the recommended range. Sleep falls to 7.9 hours with one to three hours of screen time and to 7.1 hours with three to five. Teenagers who use screens for more than five hours sleep only 6.2 hours. Thus, the more time teenagers spend on screens at night, the less they sleep, which supports the writer’s warning in Wired Awake.

Age15–1920–2425–3435 and over
Check first (%)14192738
Question. What conclusion can be drawn from the table? Explain in 4–5 sentences, using evidence.
The table shows the percentage of people who check a story before sharing it, by age group. Only 14 percent of people aged 15 to 19 check first, and the figure rises to 19 percent for those aged 20 to 24. It reaches 27 percent for people aged 25 to 34 and 38 percent for those aged 35 and over. Thus, the younger people are, the less likely they are to check a story, which supports the writer’s call for media literacy in schools and universities.
