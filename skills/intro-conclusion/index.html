Introductions and conclusions

The first and last paragraphs have jobs. Name the technique the writer uses, then say what it does for the reader.

How it’s markedThe official split

For the 2018 SE question the official answer named the type (an anecdote) and two functions, marked 0.5 for the type; two functions are required, 0.25 each. The functions it accepted: attracting the reader, introducing the topic, and achieving credibility through a real-life example.

The methodHow to answer

Ten types of introduction (from the course notes):

TypeWhat it does
AnecdoteA brief story that illustrates the topic.
Facts or statisticsStriking numbers from a credible source.
HistoricalGeneral historical background.
QuestionsA series of questions that the essay then answers.
General to specificStarts wide, narrows to the thesis.
Dialogue or dramatic sceneCharacters speaking; we are dropped into a moment.
SummarySums up the whole text to arouse curiosity.
DescriptionA vivid picture of a person or place.
Listing examplesSeveral examples that point to the theme.
QuotationA famous saying that expresses the topic.

Functions to choose from: attract the reader’s attention and interest; introduce the topic; give background information; present the thesis; achieve credibility (with a real example or figure).

Five types of conclusion: result, restatement, summary, prediction, recommendation.

Intro and conclusion together are often linked: problem → solution, question → answer, past → present, or a story that returns at the end (a frame).

See it doneWorked answers

Question. What type of introduction does the writer of Wired Awake use? What two purposes does it serve?
The writer uses an anecdote: the story of Karim, a seventeen-year-old in Saida who is still watching videos at 1:40 a.m. First, it attracts the reader’s attention, since many teenagers and parents will recognise the scene. Second, it introduces the topic of the selection, how screens take over teenagers’ sleep, which the last sentence turns into the thesis.

0.5 the type, 0.25 + 0.25 two functions.

Question. What type of introduction does the writer of The Price of a Click use? What two purposes does it serve?
The writer uses an anecdote: Rana, a nineteen-year-old student in Tripoli, shares a dramatic photo of a bridge that never collapsed. First, it attracts the reader, who has probably shared a post just as quickly. Second, it introduces the topic, the spread of false news through social media, and gives it credibility through a real-life example.

0.5 the type, 0.25 + 0.25 two functions.
