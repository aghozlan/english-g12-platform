Essay toolkit

Every essay on the paper, whatever its genre, is built and marked the same way. Learn the shape once and you can use it for any prompt.

How it’s markedThree criteria

CriterionMarksWhat the examiner looks at
Content and organisation of ideas3.5Does it answer the prompt (genre, task, focus)? A clear thesis, a topic sentence per paragraph, enough support, a real conclusion, a logical order.
Language and style3.5Grammar, spelling, punctuation; connectors; precise, formal vocabulary; varied sentences.
Tidiness and legible handwriting1Clear paragraphs, neat writing, a clean final copy.
Language carries as many marks as ideas. A simple essay with clean, correct sentences beats an ambitious one full of errors.

The methodDecode the prompt first

Every prompt hides four pieces of information. Tap each to see it in a real prompt.

Some people believe that technology stands behind many dangerous diseases; however, others believe that technology has helped doctors diagnose and treat many diseases. Where do you stand? Write a well-organized argumentative essay of 250-300 words in which you show your point of view. Make sure that your essay supports your position and refutes the opposite point of view.LS, June 2019

The shapeThe official outline

The 2019 papers printed the outline the Ministry expects. Whatever the genre, it has the same frame:

  1. Introduction
    • A general statement or hook: a question, a striking fact, a short anecdote, a quotation.
    • A narrower or background statement that leads to your topic.
    • The thesis statement: one complete sentence that gives your position or main idea and announces your points.
  2. Body paragraphs (two or three)
    • A topic sentence with one controlling idea.
    • Two or three pieces of support: details, examples, facts.
    • A concluding sentence.
  3. Conclusion
    • Restate the thesis in new words.
    • A final thought: a recommendation, a prediction or a call to act.
A thesis statement is a complete sentence, gives an opinion or idea rather than a bare fact, and is never a question. It usually ends the introduction.

Word budget250–300 words, shared out

Intro ~50Body 1 ~90Body 2 ~90End ~50

With three body paragraphs, give each about 60 words. Plan for about 280: it leaves room to add or cut.

ConnectorsThe words that hold an essay together

JobConnectors
First pointFirst, To begin with, First of all, To start with,
Further pointMoreover, Furthermore, In addition, What is more, Also, Another point to be made is that
Final pointFinally, Lastly,
Someone else’s viewSome people believe that… According to… It is said that… Some argue that…
ContrastHowever, On the contrary, Conversely, Nevertheless,
Cause and resultbecause, since, As a result, Consequently, Therefore,
ConclusionIn conclusion, To sum up, All in all, In a nutshell,

Use them precisely and sparingly: one per sentence at most, and not the same one twice in a paragraph.

LanguageFive quick checks before you copy out

  1. No contractions: do not, not don’t; it is, not it’s.
  2. One tense unless time changes: an essay about today stays in the present.
  3. Subject–verb agreement: technology has, people have.
  4. Formal words: children, not kids; many, not a lot of.
  5. Mix your sentences: some short, some long, a question if it helps.
