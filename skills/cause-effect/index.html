Cause-effect essay

Why does something happen, or what does it lead to? Give each cause (or each effect) its own paragraph, and link every claim to its result.

On the paperReal prompts

The climate has become hotter than usual … Write an expository essay of 250-300 words in which you discuss two to three reasons behind the current critical situation of the environment, and then provide some effective actions humans should take in order to save our planet.LS, July 2019, second session
Artificial intelligence … is getting smarter every day. In a well-organized expository essay of 250-300 words, develop the statement above, shedding light on two positive and/or negative effects of artificial intelligence in any field(s) of your choice.LS, June 2018

How it’s markedContent 3.5, language 3.5, tidiness 1

Past papers often call these prompts expository, but the task inside is cause or effect: reasons behind, effects of. Read the task, not only the label. Note that the 2019 prompt also asked for actions to take: an answer with causes only would miss part of the content mark.

The outlineFour or five paragraphs, each with one job

  1. Introduction
    • Hook and background
    • Thesis naming your two or three causes (or effects)
  2. Body 1, 2 (and 3)
    • One cause (or effect) per paragraph: topic sentence, explanation, example
  3. Conclusion
    • Restatement
    • Final thought. If the prompt asks for actions, give them a paragraph of their own before the conclusion.

Block organisation, from the course notes: “each of the causes or effects in a separate paragraph”.

Expository essays in past papers. Until 2019 about a third of the prompts said “expository”. The 2026–2027 list names persuasive, cause-effect, problem-solution and argumentative instead. If an expository prompt appears, use the official expository outline: introduction, two body paragraphs each with a topic sentence and three pieces of evidence, conclusion. It is the same shape as the persuasive outline.

A warming world

Model essay written for this platform, 262 words.

Summers in Lebanon are getting longer and hotter, and storms are becoming more violent. Around the world, crops are failing and rivers are drying up. This critical change in the climate is not a mystery: it is mainly caused by burning fossil fuels, destroying forests and wasting resources.

The main cause is the burning of fossil fuels. Cars, power plants and factories burn oil, coal and gas, and release huge amounts of carbon dioxide into the air. This gas traps the sun’s heat around the Earth like a blanket. As a result, temperatures keep rising year after year.

Another important cause is deforestation. Trees absorb carbon dioxide and keep the soil cool and moist. However, millions of trees are cut down every year for timber, farming and building. Consequently, there are fewer trees to clean the air, and the land becomes drier and more exposed to fires and floods.

Finally, our habits of consumption make the problem worse. We waste food, water and electricity, and we buy more products than we need. Each product must be made, packed and transported, which burns even more energy and produces even more pollution. A single plastic bottle, for instance, may travel thousands of kilometres before it is thrown away after a few minutes.

To conclude, the climate crisis is the result of human choices: the fuels we burn, the forests we destroy and the way we consume. The good news is that choices can change. Clean energy, protected forests and careful habits can still slow the damage and leave the next generation a healthier planet.

TechniqueCause and effect connectors

  1. Cause → effect: As a result, Consequently, Therefore, This leads to
  2. Effect ← cause: because, since, due to, is caused by
  3. Order of importance: The main cause… Another important cause… Finally…
