In the following selection, the writer describes a small library in a Lebanese village that lends seeds instead of books. Read it carefully, and then answer the questions that follow.

The Seed Library

Original practice text written for this platform. The village and people are invented.

Every Saturday morning, a line forms outside the old school in the mountain village of Kfar Zeit. The people waiting are not there for books. They are there for seeds.

The idea began with Hiba Sleiman, a retired biology teacher who noticed that the farmers around her were buying expensive imported seeds every season. “Our grandparents never bought seeds,” she remembers. “They kept the best ones from each harvest and shared them with the neighbours.” She decided to bring that habit back.

The library works like any other. Members borrow a packet of seeds in spring, and after the harvest they return twice as many seeds as they took. Each packet carries a small card that describes the plant, its season and the family who grew it. Today, the shelves hold more than 140 local varieties, from mountain thyme to a small, sweet tomato that had almost disappeared.

Local seeds matter because they have adapted to the region over hundreds of years. They need less water and fewer chemicals than many imported types, a real advantage as summers grow hotter and drier. Moreover, when farmers save their own seeds, they no longer depend on prices set far away.

The library has also brought people together. Older farmers teach teenagers how to dry and store seeds, while students design the cards and run the library’s online page. “I used to think farming was for my grandfather’s generation,” says Jad, sixteen. “Now I see it is about our future.”

Hiba hopes that other villages will follow. “A seed is a small thing,” she says, “but it holds a whole history inside. When we share it, we share that history too.”

Exam questions

Sections A, B, E, F: 7.5 marks, about 30 minutes
30:00

A. Answer each question in 1–4 complete sentences using your own words.

1.Based on paragraph 2, identify two reasons that led Hiba to start the seed library.(01)
First, she saw that local farmers were paying a lot for foreign seeds every season. Second, she wanted to revive the old custom of keeping the best seeds and sharing them with neighbours.0.5 for each.
2.Scan paragraph 3 for two rules that members of the library follow.(01)
Members borrow seeds in spring, and after the harvest they must give back double the amount they borrowed.0.5 for each rule. Accept: each packet comes with a card describing the plant.
3.Based on paragraph 4, explain two advantages of local seeds.(01)
Since local seeds have adjusted to the region for centuries, they use less water and fewer chemicals, which matters as summers become hotter. Also, farmers who keep their own seeds are no longer controlled by foreign prices.0.5 for each advantage.
4.Based on paragraph 5, infer how Jad’s view of farming has changed.(01)
Jad used to see farming as an old-fashioned activity for older people. Through the library, he now sees it as important for his own generation’s future.0.5 for the old view, 0.5 for the new one.

B. The following statements are false. Rewrite them correctly.

1.Hiba’s grandparents bought new seeds every season.(0.5)
Hiba’s grandparents never bought seeds; they kept the best ones from each harvest.
2.Local seeds need more water than many imported types.(0.5)
Local seeds need less water than many imported types.
